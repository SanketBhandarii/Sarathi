# Demo video script

**Limit: 5 minutes.** Screen recording with voiceover. You do not need to be on camera.

Must cover: the problem · who it is for · why it matters · the project working end to end.

Before you record: sign up a fresh account so the whole flow is real, and have the IBPS PO PDF open in a second tab at page 6.

---

## 0:00 to 0:35 — the problem

*(on screen: the landing page)*

> A student in India sits one government exam a year. They fight paperwork all year.
>
> Thirty lakh people apply for one SSC exam. Meanwhile RBI Grade B and NABARD take a fraction of that, because far fewer people have heard of them.
>
> And when a student does find an exam, they still don't know if they qualify. Eligibility is five rules multiplied together. Age. Category relaxation. Qualification level. Domicile. Attempts already used.
>
> Most find out they were never eligible **after** they have paid the fee.

## 0:35 to 0:55 — who it is for, and why it matters

*(scroll the landing page slowly)*

> This is for one person. Ravi, twenty one, from Nagpur. OBC. Studying eight hours a day in a rented room.
>
> He is eligible for exams he has never heard of. And if he misses one form, he waits a year. Not a week. A year.

## 0:55 to 1:35 — sign up, and the thing most products get wrong

*(sign up live, enter the emailed code)*

> He gives an email, confirms it with a real code, and then fills his details once.

*(reach the education step, add levels)*

> Here is a decision worth explaining. Sarathi does not ask "what is your degree". It asks level by level, from tenth upward.
>
> Because SSC MTS and India Post GDS need only tenth. Junior Engineer needs a diploma. CGL needs a degree. A student with only tenth qualifies for three of those and has no degree at all. One "degree" box would give them the wrong answer.

*(enter a CGPA, let the conversion appear)*

> And when he enters a CGPA, it shows him the percentage the form will ask for, and tells him to use his university's own conversion if it differs.

## 1:35 to 2:35 — the verdict, and the proof

*(dashboard loads)*

> Now it goes to work. Seventeen exams, in four layers. Central government. His own state. His own city. And other states that take applicants from anywhere, which almost nobody looks for.

*(open the IBPS PO row)*

> "You get three extra years because you are OBC."

*(point at the citation)*

> And underneath, page six, the commission's own sentence. Not our claim. Theirs.

*(switch to the PDF tab, scroll to page 6, then back)*

> That is the whole promise. **Every fact traces to a line in an official PDF.** If Sarathi cannot find the line, it does not state the fact.

*(open a blocked exam)*

> And it says no properly. "This exam needs graduation. Your highest is twelfth standard." With the clause. Because being told no today is worth more than a rejection in three months.

## 2:35 to 3:15 — the part that makes it an agent

*(open Agent Journal)*

> Here is what Sarathi did last night. Three sources checked. Eighteen quotes re-verified against the original PDFs. Forty six rules evaluated.
>
> Messages sent to Ravi: **zero**.

*(point at the two rows side by side)*

> Same sixty four checks the next night. This time, one message, because a form he qualifies for closes in thirteen days.
>
> Most products would send him something every day to look useful. This one does the work and stays quiet. **That is the difference between an agent and an app you have to remember to open.**

## 3:15 to 3:45 — when it is wrong

*(open "When I was wrong")*

> Indian commissions change dates after they publish. Most websites update quietly and say nothing.
>
> "I told you the last date was the eighth of September. It has moved earlier, to the second. You have less time than I said. I was wrong."
>
> With the clause from each version. An agent that corrects itself out loud.

## 3:45 to 4:15 — the boring thing that decides everything

*(open Documents, pick the SSC tab)*

> Forms reject a photo that is one kilobyte too big.
>
> SSC wants a signature at two hundred and thirty six by seventy nine pixels. IBPS wants one forty by sixty. UPSC wants it signed three times, one below the other.

*(upload a photo, show the output)*

> One phone photo in. Exact pixels and exact byte range out.

*(point at the SSC warning)*

> And this: from 2026, SSC will not accept a gallery photo at all. It has to be captured live in their portal. A student who does not know that loses the form.

## 4:15 to 4:45 — how it is built

*(show the architecture diagram)*

> Two Strands agents in a cyclic graph. A reader records what it finds. A checker calls a tool that compares every number against the page it claims to come from. If it disagrees, the work goes back to the reader.
>
> But the two checks that matter use no AI at all. Is this sentence on this page. Does this number appear in this sentence.
>
> We tested that by planting errors. Maximum age thirty changed to forty five. The AI verifier missed it. The arithmetic caught it instantly.
>
> **So no model decides who is eligible. The agents read the document. The arithmetic is code.**

## 4:45 to 5:00 — close

*(back to the dashboard)*

> Sarathi is free for students, permanently. No fee, no advertising, no paid data source.
>
> A student studies. Sarathi carries the paperwork.

---

## Things to get right

- **Show the PDF.** The moment you switch to the real notification and find the quoted line on page six is the most convincing five seconds in the whole video.
- **Let the silence land.** Pause on "messages sent: zero" for a full beat.
- **Do not read the numbers out.** Let the screen carry them.
- **Record in English.** Show the Hindi toggle once, briefly, and move on.
- **Do not apologise for the MPSC rows.** When you reach them, say the honest line: "these are scanned photographs of paper, so Sarathi refuses to guess and hands the student the official notification instead."

## Things to avoid

- Do not open with "we aggregate exam notifications." That sounds like every job website and a judge will file you next to them.
- Do not claim it watches every exam in India. Five sources done properly is the stronger and truer claim.
- Do not skip the citation. It is the whole product.
